<p class="page-intro">I study how intelligent systems can learn useful structure from imperfect observations and human demonstrations, then turn that structure into robust prediction and action.</p>

## Dexterous manipulation from human demonstrations

<div class="research-detail">
  <div class="research-meta">
    <span>TEA Lab</span><span>Oct. 2025 — May 2026</span>
  </div>
  <h3>DexReprise</h3>
  <p>At the Tsinghua Embodied AI Lab, advised by Prof. Huazhe Xu, I worked on a reference-guided framework that converts offline human demonstrations—including motion capture and video—into closed-loop dexterous robot policies, removing the need for real-time teleoperation.</p>
  <p>My core contribution focused on the distillation pipeline. I designed a dual-objective spatial-awareness auxiliary task: an observation loss for retaining latent 3D coordinates and a relative delta loss for modeling geometric discrepancy. I contributed across the full research cycle and co-authored the resulting CoRL 2026 submission.</p>

## Belief-factorized world models

<div class="research-detail">
  <div class="research-meta">
    <span>LIU Lab</span><span>May 2026 — Present</span>
  </div>
  <h3>Separating inference, memory, and dynamics</h3>
  <p>At LIU Lab, advised by Prof. Ziming Liu, I investigate whether the responsibilities of a world model should be learned jointly or assigned to distinct modules: inferring hidden state, maintaining belief over what is currently unobserved, and evolving physics forward in time.</p>
  <p>I built controlled toy-physics testbeds—including Kepler orbits and occluded billiards—to test this factorization under partial observability. The broader goal is to understand when explicit modular structure improves reasoning, interpretability, and generalization.</p>
